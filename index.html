Research

Planetary instrumentation · Caltech and JPL · 2022–

Miniaturized Mössbauer spectrometer

I work with JPL on a NASA PICASSO prototype that combines backscatter Mössbauer spectroscopy with X-ray fluorescence to identify iron-bearing minerals and a rock’s composition in place. My part is working out what limits the spectra: how wide the lines get and how long a measurement takes. I measured the piezo drive’s motion with a laser vibrometer, compared sensors for its feedback loop, debugged the counting electronics, and use a Monte Carlo model of photon paths to compare detector layouts.

Alloy thermodynamics · Caltech · 2022–

The entropy of high-entropy alloys

High-entropy alloys are named for the entropy of mixing several elements, but atomic vibrations, electrons, and magnetism add entropy too. I measured the heat capacity of three alloy families from 2 to 400 K, with and without added aluminum, and used diffraction and microscopy to estimate the mixing part. At 385 K, those three measured contributions together were about three times the entropy of mixing. Adding aluminum lowered the total in all three families.

Simulation · Mössbauer spectrometer

Simulating photon paths in a Mössbauer spectrometer

My Monte Carlo model follows photons from the source to the sample and on to the detector. Photons that travel at an angle to the drive’s motion pick up a smaller Doppler shift, which broadens the spectral lines. The layout sets both the count rate and that broadening, so I compare designs in the model before we change the hardware. It predicts that moving the source back in the next design doubles the count rate at the same line sharpness.

Thermodynamics · machine-learned potentials

Faster entropy estimates with a downloaded force field

Fitting a machine-learned potential for each material takes weeks of quantum-mechanical calculations. Pretrained models built from existing databases skip that step and get most of the way there. I test how far one, MACE-OMAT-0, goes on the entropy of melting by repeating a study from our group that used custom-fitted models.

Battery materials · Georgia Tech, 2019–2022

Solid electrolytes and Li-ion cathodes

In Gleb Yushin’s lab, I changed how we milled a lithium-rich anti-perovskite electrolyte and raised its ionic conductivity roughly tenfold. In a second project, I used a common shampoo ingredient to make pores in nickel-manganese-cobalt oxide (NMC) cathodes. At 3C, a 20-minute discharge rate, the porous coating delivered about 17% more capacity.
