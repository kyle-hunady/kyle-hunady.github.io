I'm a materials science PhD candidate at Caltech, in Brent Fultz's group.

Most of my work is on entropy in alloys. A high-entropy alloy is named for the idea that mixing many elements buys enough configurational entropy — kBln(N) — to stabilize a solid solution. I measure the total entropy by precision calorimetry from 2 to 400 K across Al-added CoCrFeNi, CoCrFeMnNi and NbTa0.5TiZr, and that picture does not predict the trends. Vibrational entropy dominates, and together with B2 ordering it can overwhelm the configurational gain from adding a component.

The caveat matters: those Al-rich ordered states still form. They are driven by Al–transition-metal bonding enthalpy, which the entropy changes oppose rather than determine — so measuring the entropy of the phase that forms does not by itself settle phase stability.

The rest is instrumentation. With JPL I work on a miniaturized 57Fe Mössbauer/XRF spectrometer for planetary surfaces. That covers the piezoelectric drive and its feedback loop, the multichannel scaler firmware, and the detector pulse processing.

Before Caltech I was at Georgia Tech, where I worked on battery processing in Gleb Yushin's lab and taught in the makerspaces. Outside the lab I lead group hikes for the Caltech Y and the Alpine Club, do a lot of 3D printing, and write small tools when I need one.

Simulation

Monte Carlo geometry search

A photon ray-tracer that grid-searches source–sample–detector geometries for the backscatter instrument. Count rate and spectral fidelity pull against each other: the angular spread that a wider aperture buys also smears the velocity axis, because a photon leaving at an angle sees only the cosine of the drive velocity. The simulator measures that smearing as a kernel, so a fit to real data can be corrected for the geometry it was taken in.

The entropy of high-entropy alloys

High-entropy alloys are named for their configurational entropy. We measured the total entropy of Al-added transition-metal alloys and found ideal mixing does not predict the trends — vibrational entropy and B2 ordering can overwhelm the configurational gain, leaving bonding enthalpy to decide what forms.

SCA windows for gas-filled counters

Reads a shaped pulse train off a Siglent oscilloscope and builds the pulse-height distribution, so the single-channel analyzer window can be set where the counts actually are. Picking the window from the measured distribution instead of by eye improves counting efficiency a great deal.
