Research

Materials at the edge of order

My work lives at the intersection of thermodynamics, spectroscopy, and extreme environments — from alloys that embrace disorder as a design principle to instruments built to answer questions about planets we've never stood on.

Current Work
Project 01 · Materials Thermodynamics

Entropy of High-Entropy Alloys

High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are multi-principal-element alloys — materials with five or more elements in roughly equal proportions — that derive their stability from configurational entropy rather than from a single dominant element.

My research probes the thermodynamic mechanisms that govern phase stability in these alloys: when does entropy actually win? How do local chemical ordering and short-range interactions complicate the simple "maximum entropy" picture? I use a combination of computational methods and experimental characterization to push on the theory.

Project 02 · Instrumentation · Space Science

Mössbauer Spectrometer Miniaturization for Mars

Mössbauer spectroscopy is one of the most powerful tools for identifying iron-bearing minerals — which makes it uniquely valuable for understanding Mars' geological history and the potential for past habitability.

The challenge: existing Mössbauer instruments are too large and power-hungry for next-generation Mars landers and rovers with tight mass and power budgets. My work focuses on miniaturizing the spectrometer hardware without sacrificing the sensitivity needed to do real science on the Martian surface.
