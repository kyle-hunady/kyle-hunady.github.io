Research

Entropy, and an instrument for Mars

Two projects. One measures where the entropy in a high-entropy alloy actually comes from. The other shrinks a Mössbauer spectrometer until it fits on a Mars lander.

Current Work
Project 01 · Materials Thermodynamics

Entropy of High-Entropy Alloys

High-entropy alloys mix five or more elements in near-equal amounts. The premise of the field is that configurational entropy, the number of ways those atoms can be arranged, is what stabilizes the single-phase solid solution.

I measure that entropy instead of assuming it. Heat capacity from 2 K to 400 K gives the absolute entropy of Al-doped CoCrFe(Mn)Ni and NbTa0.5TiZr. Adding aluminum lowers it, which is the opposite of what the configurational argument predicts. That manuscript is under review.

Project 02 · Instrumentation · Space Science

Mössbauer Spectrometer Miniaturization for Mars

Mössbauer spectroscopy identifies iron-bearing minerals and the oxidation state of the iron in them. On Mars that covers most of the interesting geology, which is why the technique flew on both Spirit and Opportunity.

Those instruments are too heavy and draw too much power for the mass budget of the next generation of landers. I'm shrinking the hardware while keeping enough signal to do real mineralogy: the velocity drive, the source geometry and the detector each have to get smaller without costing sensitivity.
