About

Kyle Hunady

PhD candidate in Materials Science at Caltech, expected 2027.

Background

I grew up taking things apart: appliances, recipes, whatever was around. Materials science fit because it sits inside everything else. I started research as a freshman at Georgia Tech by answering a cold email from a grad student who needed help, which put me in a Li-ion battery lab and left me with the question I still work on. How do atoms arrange themselves, and what happens at the boundary between order and disorder?

That question brought me to Caltech, where I work on two problems that look unrelated and share most of their physics: the thermodynamics of high-entropy alloys, and a Mössbauer spectrometer small enough to fly to another planet.

Outside the lab I write code, run student organizations, and make latte art. I've photographed every attempt since August 2025. The longitudinal record is more interesting than I expected.

Leadership & Service

Roles & involvement

Roles where the job is mostly making other people's work easier.

EAS Graduate Student Advisory Board President · Caltech

Liaison between graduate students in the Division of Engineering and Applied Sciences and division administration. Scope covers community events, mentorship awards, and climate survey feedback to improve the graduate experience. Delegates across a team of 10.

Graduate Student Council Strategic Communications Chair · Caltech

Manages the mailing list for all Caltech graduate students and coordinates how organizations and the university advertise events to the broader community. Represents the council in external and professional communications.

Molecular Gastronomists President · Georgia Tech

Ran a culinary science club. Organized cooking and baking events in communal kitchens, and gave short lectures on the science behind what we were making — tempered chocolate, protein denaturation, emulsification. Good excuse to make food and explain why it works.

HIVE & MILL Makerspaces Volunteer · Georgia Tech

Volunteered at the HIVE (electrical engineering makerspace) and the MILL (Materials Innovation Learning Lab). Helped peers with their projects and learned from what they were building in the process.
