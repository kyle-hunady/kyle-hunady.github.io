How much of the entropy is mixing?

Calorimetry down to 2 K, with Mössbauer, diffraction and DFT, on three families of high-entropy alloys.

All projects

"High-entropy alloys" are named for one kind of entropy, the disorder from mixing five or so elements on one lattice. I measure the total entropy of these alloys to see how much comes from mixing and how much from atoms vibrating.

Entropy is an area under a curve

Heat capacity measured from near absolute zero, divided by temperature and integrated, gives the entropy. Most of it comes from lattice vibrations. Below is the Debye model, the simplest version of that curve, next to the textbook mixing entropy of a five-element alloy, R ln 5.

modelThe shaded area on the left is the green curve on the right. At room temperature the vibrational term is 1.7 to 3.3 times R ln 5 across the slider's range of Debye temperatures, so the vibrations cannot be ignored. Real alloys add electronic and magnetic terms and depart from the Debye curve, which is why I measure the real one.

What I did

  • Measured heat capacity from 2 to 400 K on aluminum-added CoCrFeNi, CoCrFeMnNi and NbTa₀.₅TiZr.
  • Checked phases and chemical order with X-ray diffraction, electron microscopy and ⁵⁷Fe Mössbauer spectroscopy.
  • Ran spin-polarized DFT to separate the magnetic contribution.
  • Wrote the processing code that turns raw calorimeter files into entropy with uncertainty.
